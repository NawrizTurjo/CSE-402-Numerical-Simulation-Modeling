SHORT QUESTIONS AND ANSWERS: RNG AND MONTE CARLO SIMULATION

1. Does passing the Chi-Square test necessarily mean that a generator is good?

No. Passing the test only means that the frequencies in the selected bins are
close to a uniform distribution. The numbers may still be predictable, have a
short period, or be correlated. Other statistical tests are also necessary.


2. What is a pseudo-random number generator (PRNG)?

A PRNG is a deterministic algorithm that uses a starting seed to produce a
sequence that looks random. The same seed always produces the same sequence.


3. What is a seed?

A seed is the initial value given to a pseudo-random number generator. It
determines the sequence produced by the generator.


4. Why are pseudo-random numbers useful in simulation?

They are fast to generate and reproducible. Reproducibility helps us repeat an
experiment, test a program, and compare results.


5. What is the main weakness of the Middle-Square method?

It can quickly enter a short cycle or become zero. After reaching zero, it
continues generating zero forever. Its quality also depends heavily on the seed.


6. Why are leading zeros important in the Middle-Square method?

The square must always be treated as an 8-digit value so that the correct middle
four positions are selected. Missing leading zeros changes those positions.


7. What is the Linear Congruential Generator (LCG)?

It generates integers using:

X(next) = (a * X(current) + c) mod m

The generated integer is commonly divided by m to obtain a value in [0, 1).


8. What is the difference between mixed and multiplicative LCG?

A mixed LCG uses a nonzero increment c. A multiplicative LCG uses c = 0.


9. What is the period of a random-number generator?

The period is the number of generated values before the sequence begins to
repeat. A longer period is generally better for large simulations.


10. Why does an LCG eventually repeat?

An LCG has only a finite number of possible states. Once a previous state occurs
again, all later values repeat in the same order.


11. What qualities should a good random-number generator have?

It should have a long period, an approximately uniform distribution, low
correlation, few visible patterns, good speed, and reproducible results.


12. What is the null hypothesis in a uniformity test?

H0: The generated numbers follow a uniform distribution over [0, 1).


13. What does significance level alpha = 0.05 mean?

It means that we accept a 5 percent risk of rejecting the null hypothesis when
it is actually true.


14. How is a Chi-Square decision made?

If p-value < 0.05, reject H0. Otherwise, do not reject H0.


15. What does "do not reject H0" mean?

It means there is not enough evidence to say that the values are non-uniform.
It does not prove that the generator is perfectly uniform or truly random.


16. Why is the expected count 100 for 1,000 values and 10 bins?

For a uniform distribution, every bin has equal probability. Therefore:

Expected count = 1000 / 10 = 100.


17. What does the Chi-Square statistic measure?

It measures the difference between observed and expected bin counts. A large
value shows a large difference and gives more evidence against H0.


18. What are the degrees of freedom for 10 bins?

Degrees of freedom = number of bins - 1 = 10 - 1 = 9.


19. What is the difference between Chi-Square and K-S tests?

Chi-Square compares counts in intervals, while the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test
compares the empirical cumulative distribution with the expected cumulative
distribution. The K-S test does not require dividing the data into bins.


20. What does an autocorrelation test check?

It checks whether values in different positions of a sequence are related. A
good random sequence should have autocorrelation close to zero.


21. Can uniformly distributed values still be dependent?

Yes. Values may have correct frequencies but still follow a predictable order.
Therefore, uniformity and independence must be tested separately.


22. What is Monte Carlo simulation?

Monte Carlo simulation uses repeated random samples to estimate a numerical
result or study a system containing uncertainty.


23. Why does Monte Carlo accuracy usually improve with more samples?

Random errors tend to average out as the number of samples increases. However,
improvement is slow, so a much larger sample may be needed for high accuracy.


24. How is pi estimated using Monte Carlo simulation?

Generate points (x, y) in a unit square and count those satisfying
x^2 + y^2 <= 1. Then use:

pi estimate = 4 * points inside / total points.


25. How does Monte Carlo integration work?

It evaluates the function at many randomly selected points and uses the average
function value multiplied by the length of the integration interval.


26. What is the inverse transform method?

It converts a uniform random value U into another distribution by applying the
inverse cumulative distribution function: X = F^(-1)(U).


27. What is the acceptance-rejection method?

It generates a candidate value and accepts it only when another random point
falls below the target density curve. Rejected candidates are discarded.


28. What is importance sampling?

Importance sampling draws more samples from important regions of the function
and uses weights to correct the estimate. It can reduce Monte Carlo variance.


29. What is the Metropolis-Hastings algorithm?

It is an MCMC algorithm that builds a Markov chain whose long-run samples follow
a target distribution. Each proposed value is accepted or rejected using an
acceptance probability.


30. What is the Metropolis-Hastings acceptance probability?

For a symmetric proposal, it is:

min(1, target density at proposal / target density at current value).

For a non-symmetric proposal, the proposal probabilities must also be included.


31. Why can Metropolis-Hastings accept a worse proposal?

Occasionally accepting a lower-density proposal prevents the chain from getting
stuck and allows it to explore the entire target distribution.


32. What happens when a Metropolis-Hastings proposal is rejected?

The current value is kept and added to the sample again. Repeated values are
therefore a normal part of the algorithm.


33. What is burn-in in MCMC?

Burn-in is the initial part of an MCMC sequence that is discarded because the
chain may still be strongly affected by its starting value.


34. What is a Markov chain?

It is a sequence in which the next state depends on the current state rather
than the complete history of earlier states.


35. What is the effect of proposal step size in Metropolis-Hastings?

A very small step gives high acceptance but slow movement. A very large step
causes many rejections. A suitable step size balances movement and acceptance.


36. What is the difference between deterministic and stochastic simulation?

A deterministic simulation produces the same result from fixed inputs. A
stochastic simulation contains random variables and can produce different
results on different runs.


37. What is server utilization in a queue simulation?

It is the proportion of simulation time during which the server is busy:

utilization = total busy time / total simulation time.


38. What is waiting time in a single-server FCFS queue?

It is the time from a customer's arrival until service begins:

waiting time = service start time - arrival time.


39. What does FCFS mean?

FCFS means First Come, First Served. Customers receive service in the same order
in which they arrive.


40. Why should simulation results be checked using several runs?

One run may be unusually high or low because of random variation. Several runs
provide a more reliable average and show how much the results vary.


QUESTIONS FROM THE LAST PAGES OF THE PDF

41. Why is the method called Monte Carlo?

It is named after the Monte Carlo Casino in Monaco because the method depends on
random sampling, similar to games of chance. It was developed during World War II
by scientists including Stanislaw Ulam and John von Neumann.


42. How do we estimate an expected value in Monte Carlo simulation?

Generate many independent samples and calculate their sample mean:

Y_bar = (Y1 + Y2 + ... + Yn) / n.

According to the law of large numbers, this average approaches the true expected
value as the number of samples becomes large.


43. What is the sample mean estimator?

The sample mean estimator is the average of the observed sample values. It is
used to estimate the unknown population mean or expected value:

Y_bar = (1/n) * sum(Yi).


44. Why can an integral be estimated using a sample mean?

An integral can be written as an expected value of a function of a random
variable. Therefore, we can estimate the integral by averaging the function
values obtained from many random samples.


45. Does taking more Monte Carlo samples help?

Generally, yes. As the sample size increases, the estimate usually becomes more
stable and closer to the true value because of the law of large numbers.


46. Why does Monte Carlo error not decrease monotonically?

Every estimate is calculated from random samples, so the estimate itself is a
random variable. A larger sample can occasionally produce a worse estimate than
a smaller sample, although the overall accuracy improves as sample size grows.


47. Why does the Monte Carlo method for estimating pi work?

The probability that a uniform point in the unit square falls inside the quarter
circle equals the ratio of their areas:

P(inside) = (pi/4) / 1 = pi/4.

Thus, inside points / total points is approximately pi/4, and multiplying this
ratio by 4 gives an estimate of pi.


48. Why do we check x^2 + y^2 <= 1 when estimating pi?

A point (x, y) is inside a circle of radius 1 centered at the origin when its
distance from the origin is at most 1. From the distance formula, this condition
is x^2 + y^2 <= 1.


49. Is a Monte Carlo answer exact?

No. It is a statistical estimate containing sampling error. Increasing the
number of samples usually reduces this error but does not remove randomness.


50. State the law of large numbers in the context of Monte Carlo simulation.

As the number of independent samples increases, their sample mean converges to
the true expected value. This is the main reason Monte Carlo estimation works.
